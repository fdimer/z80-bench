Screen

Click the picture and type: letters, digits and symbols come out as typed (the page presses the CPC key, with or without SHIFT, that gives that character). Backspace is DEL, Delete is CLR, Alt is COPY, the numeric keypad gives the function keys f0–f9 and its Enter is the small ENTER key. On the keyboard above, SHIFT and CTRL stay down for the next key. Load ROM takes the 464's two 16 KB images, the firmware (OS_464, CRC 815752DF) and BASIC 1.0 (CRC 7D9A3BAC), together or one at a time, or one 32 KB image of both. They are not included; your browser keeps them, so you only load them once.

The picture is built the way the machine builds it: the 6845 counts characters and lines, the gate array turns two bytes of RAM into dots for each character, in the screen mode and colours it holds at that moment, with the border outside the display and black during the sync pulses. The monitor starts a new line at every HSYNC and a new picture at every VSYNC. When the machine is paused, the white mark shows where the beam is. Sound and the cassette are not emulated yet.

Click any ringed pin for its fault menu. There are two kinds of fault. A line held high or low is a short on the wire: every pin on that line, on every chip, sees the level and shows it. A pin disconnected from its line (tick the box) is a broken trace or a lifted pin: only that chip is affected. A disconnected input floats high, or can be given a level of its own; a disconnected output no longer drives its line, which then floats high for the pins that listen to it. A disconnected pin ignores a fault on the line. Lines that reach devices not drawn as chips still act on them: an address or data line held at a level is seen by the whole machine.

Clicking the Z80's CLK pulses one clock (right-click it for its fault menu). RESET is one line for the Z80, the gate array, the 6845 and the 8255: held low, they all stay in reset while the clocks run. Without CK16 (the 16 MHz oscillator) the whole machine stops; without PHI, or with READY low, the Z80 is held while the picture goes on; without CCLK the 6845 stands still. With READY high the wait states go: RAM accesses then fall out of step with the gate array, which is modelled approximately (a read returns what the RAM last put out, a write is lost). The gate array's CPU ADDR, CAS ADDR and 244EN, and the Z80's HALT, BUSACK and RFSH, are shown only. All pins follow the machine clock by clock: the 6845's outputs change once a character (four clocks), and R, G and B show the colour at each clock. Faults are listed in the Debug panel.

Memory chips live pins · click a pin to disconnect it or hold it high or low

The machine's memory chips, from the CPC 464 circuit diagram: IC103, the 32K ROM holding the firmware and BASIC, and IC117–IC124, eight 4164 dynamic RAMs, one per data bit (IC124 is D7, IC120 is D0). Each RAM address pin is fed by a 74LS153 selector with a row bit and then a column bit of the CPU address: A0 carries A2 then A9, A1 carries A1 then A0, A2 A4/A11, A3 A3/A10, A4 A6/A13, A5 A5/A12, A6 A8/A15 and A7 A7/A14. A tile's dot lights while its chip is being accessed. Click a tile to see that chip's pins, which follow the CPU's bus cycles on every clock, wait states included; the display's own reads of the RAM, which share every microsecond with the CPU, are not shown on the pins. The gate array and the 6845 are shown beside the processor.

To simulate a fault, click a pin for its menu. Hold the whole line high or low and every pin on that line is affected, on every chip: the eight RAM chips share their address, RAS, CAS and write-enable lines, and each RAM chip's data output is one line with a data pin of the gate array. Or tick "Disconnected from the line" for a fault on this chip's pin alone. A chip with a fault is marked in red, and every fault is listed in the Debug panel, where it can be removed.

Program

Standard Zilog syntax, one instruction per line. Labels end with :; comments start with ;. Directives: ORG, EQU, DB, DW, DS, END. Numbers: 1Fh, 0x1F, $1F, %1010, 'A', and $ for the current address. Undocumented instructions (IXH, SLL, OUT (C),0) are accepted. Code starts at the Load at address; an ORG in the source overrides it. Import a file with the button or by dropping it on the editor.

Hex bytes separated by spaces or newlines. Text after ; is ignored. Write 0100: to continue loading at another address. Intel HEX files (lines starting with :) are also accepted and keep their own addresses. Raw binary ROM images (.bin, .rom) import here as hex. Memory

Scroll through all 64 KB as the CPU sees it now: ROM where a ROM half is switched on, RAM elsewhere. Click a byte to edit it (the change goes to the RAM). Blue marks the instruction being executed, amber marks bytes written by the last instruction, underline marks the top of the stack. With Follow PC ticked, each step brings the current instruction back into view; untick it to keep watching another area.

Memory bitmap

Point at the bitmap to read a byte; click to show it in Memory.

Every bit of memory is one pixel. Each byte is a row of 8 pixels (bit 7 on the left) and each strip is one 256-byte page, read top to bottom; each band holds 8K. The ROM's two halves come first, then the 64K of RAM; the tint shows what each part of the RAM is used for (the screen memory follows the 6845's start address). The red mark is the program counter and the blue mark the stack pointer, shown on the bands the CPU sees at this moment.

    CPC hardware memory · gate array · 6845 · 8255

    The gate array (IC116) decides what the CPU reads: the 32K ROM lies over the RAM at 0000h–3FFFh (firmware) and C000h–FFFFh (BASIC), and two bits of its mode register switch each half off to reach the RAM underneath. It also makes READY, which holds the Z80 in wait states so that every bus cycle lines up with the video circuit: instructions take whole microseconds. I/O devices are picked by single address lines, so the port numbers are 16 bits: 7Fxxh gate array, BCxxh–BFxxh 6845, F4xxh–F7xxh 8255. The 6845 (IC108) times the picture: its HSYNC also feeds the gate array's interrupt counter, which asks for an interrupt every 52 lines, 300 times a second. Sound and the cassette are not emulated yet.
